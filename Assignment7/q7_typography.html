The Architecture of Web Typography

Exploring font pairings, vertical rhythm, inline emphasis, and drop caps.

Typography is the visual voice of written language on the web. When designers carefully balance font weights, generous line-height, and subtle letter-spacing, reading becomes effortless. Notice how this opening paragraph uses a classic serif typeface with a decorative drop cap via ::first-letter and small-caps emphasis along its ::first-line.

Hierarchy and Contrast

A clear typographic hierarchy guides the reader's eye from headline to subheading and body copy. Using a clean sans-serif typeface for headings creates crisp contrast against serif body text. Within paragraphs, semantic inline tags add meaningful nuance: highlighted phrases draw immediate attention to key takeaways, strong statements convey structural importance, and italicized terms introduce subtle vocal inflection.

“Good typography is invisible until it is absent; great typography makes complex ideas feel intuitive.”

Finally, controlling measure (line length around 65 to 75 characters) and applying justified or left-aligned spacing prevents visual fatigue across desktop and mobile screens alike.
